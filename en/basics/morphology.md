# Termite Basics: Morphology

<p class="muted">← Back to the <a href="{{ site.base_url }}/en/basics/">Basics index</a>.</p>

<p class="lead">This page lists the parts of a termite that can actually be seen and counted: the Chinese name used on this site, the Latin/English term, the caste in which it occurs, and what to look for. Entries are compiled from the archived volumes of <i>Fauna Sinica, Insecta Vol. 17, Isoptera</i> (Huang Fusheng et al., 2000), <i>Termitology</i> (Cheng Dongbao &amp; Yang Zhaofen, 2014), <i>Biology of Termites: A Modern Synthesis</i> and Krishna et al. (2013), <i>Treatise on the Isoptera of the World</i>. Terminology follows the site glossary (<code>_data/translation_glossary.yml</code>) so that it matches the checklist, the family and genus pages, and the keys.</p>

Termites are front-headed (prognathous) insects with chewing mouthparts, divided into head, thorax and abdomen. Adults are usually a few millimetres to just over a centimetre long; alates reach 10–30 mm including the wings; a long-lived queen, with her abdomen enormously distended, may reach 60–70 mm, and a few African species exceed 100 mm. Castes of the same species differ so much that every description must state which caste is being described — the reason identification keys are always split into alates and soldiers.

This page covers external morphology and the main internal organs only; names and distributions of taxa are in the [checklist]({{ site.base_url }}/en/list/), and identification runs through the “Morphological keys” on the family and genus pages.

## 1. Castes and the two body plans {#castes}

Two body plans are traditionally recognised: the **imago** plan (reproductives and workers, close to the alate in body shape, mouthparts and antennal segmentation) and the **soldier** plan (head capsule and mandibles specialised, antennal segments fewer, eyes reduced). Within these, the castes are:

| Type | Name | Morphological notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Imago · reproductive | alate (winged reproductive) | Two long wing pairs, well-developed compound eyes and a pair of ocelli; body yellow-brown to dark brown; wings break off at the humeral suture after the dispersal flight, leaving a dealate |
| Imago · reproductive | brachypterous, apterous | Short-winged or wingless supplementary reproductives, common when the primary queen is lost |
| Imago · non-reproductive | worker | The most numerous caste; eyes reduced or absent, wingless; major and minor workers are sometimes separable |
| Soldier | soldier | Mandibles greatly enlarged (mandibulate) or head drawn out into a nasus with reduced mandibles (nasute); eyes usually reduced |
| Immature | larva, nymph | Nymphs already show wing pads and pass through several instars; body pale, cuticle thin |
| Reproductive | queen, king | The queen's abdomen becomes physogastric as the ovaries develop and the intersegmental membranes are exposed; the king changes little |

## 2. Head {#head}

The head capsule is round, oval, square or pear-shaped and prognathous. Its most conspicuous feature is the **epicranial suture**: a transverse (ecdysial) line and a median longitudinal line meeting in a T or Y. Many species show it only faintly. The area in front of the transverse line is the **frons**; behind it lies the **vertex**, and behind that the **occiput**. In Rhinotermitidae and Termitidae the junction of the two lines is often depressed or raised and opens as the **fontanelle**, the opening of the frontal gland and one of the hallmarks of Neoisoptera.

The compound eyes sit on the sides of the head. They are large in alates — about 200 ommatidia per eye in <i>Mastotermes</i>, 300–400 in <i>Coptotermes formosanus</i> and about 360 in <i>Odontotermes formosanus</i> — and reduced to a pigment patch or lost entirely in workers and soldiers. The single pair of ocelli lies above or anterodorsally to the compound eyes, whitish and translucent; they occur only in reproductives, and are missing even there in a few groups.

Antennae are moniliform or oval with 9–30+ segments; beyond the scape and pedicel, the third segment is often split, producing an “incomplete division” that must be allowed for when counting.

Mouthparts are of the chewing type: the labrum covers the mouth; the clypeus is divided into a soft anteclypeus and a sclerotised postclypeus (often with a median groove in Rhinotermitidae and Termitidae); the paired mandibles carry an apical tooth, marginal teeth and a basal molar plate; each maxilla has a cardo, stipes, galea, lacinia and a 5-segmented palp; the labium has a postmentum, prementum, glossa, paraglossa and 3-segmented palps. The mouth cavity is enclosed by the labrum, mandibles, maxillae and labium, with the hypopharynx forming its floor.

## 3. Thorax, wings and legs {#thorax}

The thorax has three segments: pro-, meso- and metathorax. The pronotum is free and shield-like, and highly variable — transverse-rectangular, semicircular, trapezoidal, or saddle-shaped with the front raised and the sides deflexed; its front and rear margins are often notched. The meso- and metanota are associated with the pterothorax. The prosternum is reduced; the mesosternum is the most developed and roughly triangular. Anterior and posterior notal wing processes connect to the wing base through the axillary sclerites.

There are two pairs of membranous, elongate wings, folded flat over the abdomen at rest, the forewing usually a little longer than the hindwing — hence “Isoptera”, although they are not truly equal. Wing vestiture differs by family: wings are bare (or with microtrichia only) in Mastotermitidae, Kalotermitidae and Hodotermitidae, hairy in <i>Coptotermes</i>. Mastotermitidae and Hodotermitidae have a reticulate membrane with fine cross-veins; only Mastotermitidae have a large anal lobe on the hindwing. After the dispersal flight the wings break off along the humeral (basal) suture, leaving triangular **wing scales**: the forewing scale is distinctly larger than the hindwing scale in Kalotermitidae and Rhinotermitidae, but about equal in Termitidae.

The three pairs of legs have short coxae and trochanters and long femora and tibiae; the tibia bears a row of stout spines laterally and 2–3 apical spurs, summarised as the tibial spur formula (3:3:3, 3:2:2, 2:2:2 …). The tarsus is usually 4-segmented; 5 in Mastotermitidae, 5 ventrally but 4 dorsally in Archotermopsidae (tarsomere 2 reduced and visible only ventrally), and 3 in <i>Stylotermes</i> and <i>Indotermes</i>. The pretarsus ends in a pair of curved claws; some Kalotermitidae have an arolium.

## 4. Abdomen {#abdomen}

The abdomen has 10 segments, cylindrical to olive-shaped, with tergites and sternites joined by membrane so that it can expand — this is how a queen's abdomen becomes physogastric. Spiracles occur on segments 1–8; sternite 1 is reduced or lost. Tergite 10 tapers and overhangs the anus as the supra-anal plate (epiproct); sternite 10 is paired and divided medially as the paraprocts. In females sternite 7 is enlarged, rounded behind and covers sternites 8 and 9 as the subgenital plate. The male gonopore opens between sternites 9 and 10, and in primitive groups sternite 9 carries a pair of unsegmented styli. A pair of cerci lies beside sternite 10: usually 2 segments, but 5–8 in Mastotermitidae and some primitive taxa. An ovipositor (three pairs of valves) occurs only in female Mastotermitidae.

Reproductives often show brownish patches on the abdomen bearing short, erect yellow **secretory setae**; their secretion attracts workers to lick it and may play a role in caste formation. The sternal gland lies at the base of sternite 5 in workers of Kalotermitidae, Rhinotermitidae and Termitidae, on sternites 3–5 in Mastotermitidae, and on sternite 4 in Hodotermitidae and Archotermopsidae.

## 5. Soldier-specific structures {#soldier}

Soldiers are the “soldier plan”: head capsule and mandibles highly specialised, antennal segments often fewer, compound eyes reduced to traces. Two main types are recognised. **Mandibulate soldiers** have enormous mandibles and a head capsule that does not project forward; the inner teeth of the left and right mandibles often differ, and in some genera (e.g. <i>Capritermes</i>) the mandibles are twisted into a strongly asymmetric “snapping” bite. **Nasute soldiers** have reduced mandibles and a head drawn out into a long nasus, with the fontanelle opening at its tip and ejecting a sticky secretion (e.g. <i>Nasutitermes</i>). A few genera are intermediate, such as <i>Armitermes</i>, sometimes called nasutoid.

Soldier heads are square, oblong, round, oval, pear-shaped or conical. In <i>Cryptotermes</i> the head is short, thick and raised in front, plugging the gallery opening — phragmosis. The epicranial suture is often Y-shaped or reduced to the median line. The frons may be flat or raised, and in nasute soldiers it becomes the nasus. The fontanelle varies: absent in many lower termites; a large circular pore on the front of the head in <i>Coptotermes</i>, which secretes a milky acidic fluid (hence the Chinese name “milk-exuding termite”); at the nasus tip in <i>Nasutitermes</i>. Labral shape is a standard character: tongue-shaped in lower groups, bifid or asymmetric in Termitidae, and extremely long and grooved in <i>Schedorhinotermes</i>, where it can cover the mandible tips and the groove leads to the fontanelle.

The ratio of pronotal width to head width is also used: the pronotum is wider than or about as wide as the head in Kalotermitidae, slightly narrower in Rhinotermitidae, and much narrower in Termitidae. In <i>Reticulitermes</i> soldiers, the number of setae in the middle of the pronotum separates the “raised-frons” group (more than 20 setae) from the “flat-frons” group (about 10 or none).

## 6. Internal organs {#internal}

The gut has three regions. The foregut comprises the mouth, pharynx, esophagus, crop and **proventriculus** (gizzard): its chitinous lining bears longitudinal ridges, each with transverse rows of palmate processes carrying 8–10 spines pointing backwards to prevent regurgitation; behind it lies the stomodeal valve. The midgut runs from the stomodeal valve to the Malpighian tubules, lined by columnar and regenerative cells with a brush border, and is enclosed by the **peritrophic membrane**, a protein mesh that protects the epithelium and acts as an ultrafilter. The hindgut runs from the Malpighian tubules to the anus; its anterior part dilates into the gourd-shaped **paunch**, thin-walled and housing symbiotic flagellates and bacteria, followed by the colon and rectum with six parallel **rectal pads**.

In Termitidae the hindgut is further modified: Malpighian tubules are fewer, the proventriculus simplified, midgut caeca lost, and a **mixed segment** of mid- and hindgut epithelium appears between midgut and P1, with the Malpighian tubules clustered nearby as a **Malpighian knot**; a sclerotised **enteric valve** forms between P1 and P3. Hindgut compartments follow Holmgren (1909): the enteric valve is P2, P1 (ileum) precedes it, and P3 (paunch), P4 (colon) and P5 (rectum) follow. In lower termites P3 is the largest compartment and is packed with flagellates; Termitidae lack hindgut flagellates and rely on bacteria; Macrotermitinae and some relatives also cultivate fungus combs.

Other internal organs include the brain (proto-, deuto- and tritocerebrum) with the subesophageal ganglion, three thoracic and six abdominal ganglia forming the ventral nerve cord; paired ovaries of many ovarioles (from 7–10 per side in Kalotermitidae to several thousand in <i>Macrotermes</i>), oviducts, spermatheca and vagina; paired testes, vasa deferentia, ejaculatory duct, seminal vesicles and accessory glands; and the frontal, salivary (labial), mandibular, sternal and tergal glands.

## 7. Using these terms with the keys {#keys}

The family and genus pages of this site embed morphological keys, split into alates and soldiers. Before using one, check three things: **which caste** you have, **whether the specimen is complete** (wings, antennae, tarsi and palps break off easily), and **whether measurements follow the same standard**. The features below most often open a couplet; they are ordered from easiest to hardest.

1. **Count the tarsomeres** — 4 in most families; 5 in Mastotermitidae; 5 ventrally and 4 dorsally in Archotermopsidae; 3 in <i>Stylotermes</i> and <i>Indotermes</i>.
2. **Check the tibial spur formula** — 3:3:3, 3:2:2, 2:2:2 …, counted on all three legs.
3. **Look for the fontanelle** — present in Neoisoptera (Rhinotermitidae, Termitidae and allies), usually absent in lower termites; at the nasus tip in nasutes.
4. **Check the ocelli** — one pair, in reproductives only; do not mistake a pigment patch of a reduced compound eye for an ocellus.
5. **Measure the mandibles** — left mandibular index and tooth counts are standard at genus and species level; soldiers are often asymmetric.
6. **Count cercal segments** — usually 2; 5–8 in Mastotermitidae and some primitive taxa.
7. **Compare wing scales** — forewing scale much larger than hindwing scale (Kalotermitidae, Rhinotermitidae) or about equal (Termitidae).
8. **Check venation** — whether R and Rs are fused, whether an anal vein is present, where M arises and branches; the hindwing anal lobe is large only in Mastotermitidae.
9. **Soldier head and pronotum** — head shape (square, pear-shaped, conical) and pronotal width relative to head width are the entry points for family and genus keys.
10. **Dissect the gut** (subfamily level) — presence and position of the mixed segment, Malpighian tubule insertion and knot, P3 diverticulum, enteric valve structure.

For finer identification, enter the [checklist]({{ site.base_url }}/en/list/) and open the family or genus page; every key there cites its source, and any term can be looked up here by Chinese or Latin name.

## 8. Names, synonyms and sources {#naming}

The same structure often carries different names in different literatures; when reading a key, trust the Latin/English term. Common correspondences:

- **Postmentum / gula** — Chinese works use 后颏; older English works often write gula, although the two are not strictly homologous. Compare by position: the large plate at the base of the labium.
- **Vertex / 头盖** — Chinese textbooks call the region behind the transverse line 头盖; English usually says vertex.
- **Proventriculus / gizzard** — the same organ; the first is morphological, the second functional (also “gizzard” in Chinese 砂囊).
- **Enteric valve / P2** — P2 is the compartment number, enteric valve the morphological name.
- **Arolium / 爪间突** — the pad between the claws in some Kalotermitidae.
- **Fontanelle / frontal pore** — the same opening; the second stresses that it is the duct of the frontal gland.
