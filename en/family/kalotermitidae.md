<h2>About the family</h2>
<p>The family Kalotermitidae was established by Froggatt in 1897(16), with the type genus Kalotermes Hagen, 1853. The family name derives from the type genus, formed from the Greek kalos (beautiful) and termes. This checklist includes 23 genera and 480 species.</p>
<p>This family is commonly known as the 'drywood termites'. Most species build their nests and feed within dry wood, do not depend on the soil, and their nest is also their food source, so individuals do not leave the nest to forage over long distances. The family has no true worker caste, and nest labor is performed by pseudergates. The soldiers are of the mandibulate type, with the head roughly rectangular and lacking a fontanelle; the alates possess ocelli, by which they can be distinguished from groups that possess a fontanelle, such as the Rhinotermitidae and Termitidae. In distribution the family is pantropical to subtropical, extending into warm-temperate regions such as those with a Mediterranean climate, and it is especially diverse in dead branches and broken wood in the tropical forest canopy. Phylogenetically, this family is one of the earlier-diverging lineages among living termites (17), and it is the sister group of the Neoisoptera, so it is important for understanding the early evolution of eusociality in termites. With regard to invasion and dispersal, the drywood termites of this family are the most common wood-borne group in the tropical and subtropical world: Incisitermes minor, the western drywood termite (native to the western United States and northern Mexico, and introduced into Canada, China, Japan, Italy and Hawaii (United States)); Cryptotermes brevis, the West Indian drywood termite, is native to the desert regions of the Pacific coast of South America and has been spread by sea-borne timber to the southeastern United States, Hawaii, the Caribbean islands, South Africa, Madagascar and Australia, making it the most widely distributed invasive drywood termite species (18); Cryptotermes domesticus, the domestic drywood termite, is native to Southeast Asia and has been introduced into the coastal areas of South China, Taiwan, Japan, Australia and the Pacific islands; Cryptotermes dudleyi is native to the Oriental region and has established populations in the Neotropical Panama, Costa Rica, Colombia, Brazil and the West Indies; Cryptotermes havilandi is native to tropical Africa and has also established populations in Suriname, Guyana, Brazil and the West Indies. In addition, Cryptotermes cynocephalus Light is native to the Philippines and Southeast Asia and has been introduced into Sri Lanka, the area north of Cape York Peninsula in Australia, Rabaul in Papua New Guinea, and Hawaii (first recorded on Oahu); Glyptotermes breviconis of the genus Glyptotermes is native to the southeastern coast of Australia and has established local populations in natural trees in New Zealand and Fiji. In Sri Lanka, tea plantations are attacked by Postelectrotermes militaris and Glyptotermes dilatatus, which damage the tea plants (19); genera such as Bifiditermes, Eucryptotermes, Marginitermes, Paraneotermes, Procryptotermes and Rugitermes are minor timber pests (20).</p>
