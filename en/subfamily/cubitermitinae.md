<h2>About the subfamily</h2>
<p>The subfamily Cubitermitinae was established by Weidner in 1956, with the type genus Cubitermes Wasmann, 1902, from which the subfamily name is taken. This checklist records 28 genera and 149 species.</p>
<p>All known genera and species of this subfamily are distributed in sub-Saharan Africa — West Africa (Côte d'Ivoire, Nigeria, Guinea, Senegal, Gambia, Togo, etc.), Central Africa and the Congo Basin (Cameroon, Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Gabon, Central African Republic), East Africa (Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, Rwanda), and southern Africa (South Africa, Zimbabwe, Zambia, Namibia, Angola, etc.). The diet is mainly soil (humus). Nesting is its most distinctive feature — many genera build columnar, umbrella-shaped, or mushroom-shaped mounds on the ground surface, the nest walls being constructed of soil and feces, which can markedly concentrate soil clay and nutrients and modify soil structure (in Ugandan grassland, the density of Cubitermes testaceus mounds can reach more than a hundred per hectare (48)); they are often used as bioindicator groups of soil health. The soldiers have biting-type mandibles and defend at the nest entrance. As represented by the type genus Cubitermes, the soldier head is nearly rectangular with the fontanelle on the frons, the antennae have about 14–15 articles, and the mandibles are short, stout, and incurved. In the genera Procubitermes, Noditermes, Basidentitermes, Unguitermes, Unicornitermes, etc., the soldier abdomen is milky white (white-gutted type), the soldiers being fed purely on worker saliva. The worker ileum (P1) is inflated into a sac, the enteric valve has a median pad, the hindgut P3 bears a caecum ventrally, and the gizzard lacks a columnar belt. Taxonomically, this subfamily is a well-supported monophyletic group; the hindgut of Cubitermes is a classic material for studying the anaerobic fermentation and methanogenic microflora of the higher-termite gut (49).</p>
<p>Economically, all members of this subfamily are soil-feeding groups, and where their mounds occur densely along the edges of moist pastures they may occupy and cover land (50).</p>
