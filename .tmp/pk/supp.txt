Clade Neoisoptera Engel, Grimaldi, & Krishna
This monophyletic clade includes the families Stylotermitidae, Serritermitidae, Rhinotermitidae
sensu novo, Termitogetonidae, Psammotermitidae, Heterotermitidae, and Termitidae. The most
defining features of members in this lineage are the presence of a fontanelle (aperture of the frontal
gland) on the frons and the absence of arolium between the pretarsal claws.

Family Stylotermitidae Holmgren & Holmgren
Stylotermitinae Holmgren & Holmgren, 1917: 141. Type genus: Stylotermes Holmgren &

         Holmgren, 1917.
         Included genera (3): Parastylotermes Snyder & Emerson, Prostylotermes Engel &
Grimaldi, and Stylotermes Holmgren & Holmgren.
         Diagnosis (modified after Krishna et al.1): Termites with pseudergates (without a true
worker caste); endosymbiotic protists present; comparatively small colonies, with a relatively
diffuse nest, in living and partially dead trees.
Imago. Head nearly circular; fontanelle exceptionally small, sometimes not visible; compound
eyes small- to medium-sized; ocelli present; antennae with 16-22 articles; postclypeus quite short,
faintly arched; pronotum flat. Imago-worker mandibles: left with one apical tooth (LAt) and three
marginal teeth (LMt), second marginal tooth (LMt2) exceptionally small, shorter than first
marginal (LMt1) or apical tooth (LAt); right with an apical tooth (RAt) and two marginal teeth
(RMt), subsidiary tooth present at base of apical (RAt) and first marginal tooth (RMt1). Legs: tarsi
trimerous; tibial spur formula 3:2:2 (Stylotermes) or 2:2:2 (Parastylotermes, Prostylotermes).

anterior margin of wing; Rs sclerotized, running close to costal margin; M present, weak, running
closer to Cu than to Rs, typically simple and ending near wing apex; Cu weak, extending near to
wing apex, with several branches extending to posterior margin; anal vein absent. Hindwing: C+Sc
and Rs as in forewing; M arising from Rs immediately outside wing scale; Cu as in forewing.
Soldier. Head long and narrow; mandibles saber-shaped, with one or two, minute, toothlike
crenulations at base; fontanelle small; mandibles without prominent teeth, crenulations at inner
basal region; antennae with 13-19 articles; pronotum flat.
Pseudergate (true worker caste absent). Gut: esophagus distinct from the crop (as opposed to
broadening into the crop as in Mastotermitidae, Teletisoptera, Serritermitidae, or Kalotermitidae);

divided into columns (sclerotized, with scaly surfaces) and pulvilli (non-sclerotized), columns I
and II elongated, their length equal to that of pulvilli I, pulvilli II reduced, teeth in columns I and

Glossotermitinae Cancello & DeSouza, 2005: 35. Type genus: Glossotermes Emerson, 1950.

         Included genera (2): Glossotermes Emerson and Serritermes Wasmann.
         Diagnosis (modified after Krishna et al.1): Termites with pseudergates (without a true
worker caste); endosymbiotic protists present.

longer than wide, with sides rounded, tip hyaline. Labrum covering up well over half the length of

         Remarks: There is an interesting diversity of soldier morphologies among Rhinotermitidae
depending on genera (see in the Diagnosis above), with one to three morphs originating from
different larval instar. Some morphs are sometimes referred to as "nasutoid", owing to their long
nasus-like labrum. Hence, the monomorphic soldiers of Acorhinotermes, along with the minor
soldiers of Dolichorhinotermes, Rhinotermes, and Schedorhinotermes can be referred to as
nasutoids --although minors of Schedorhinotermes do have functional biting mandibles. On the
contrary, monomorphic soldiers of Parrhinotermes, as the major soldiers of Dolichorhinotermes,
Rhinotermes, and Schedorhinotermes have functional mandibles. Most interestingly, in the few
species of Dolichorhinotermes, Parrhinotermes, and Schedorhinotermes investigated so far, all
soldiers and workers were found to be female. This suggests that sexual specialization of neuters
might be an autapomorphy of the Rhinotermitidae sensu novo.

Family Termitidae Latreille
         Diagnosis: Termites with a true worker caste; endosymbiotic protists absent, loss of

proctodeal feeding.
Imago. Fontanelle present; Y-shaped ecdysial scar present or absent; compound eyes small to
large; ocelli present; antennae with 14-23 antennomeres; postclypeus short to long, generally
arched. Pronotum trapezoidal or sub-semicircular, slightly raised anteriorly; sometimes referred to
as "somewhat saddle-shaped" by some authors in certain genera (e.g., Emerson14). Imago-worker
mandibles with apical tooth (At) and two marginal teeth (Mt), except in some Termitinae
(Cavitermes), Crepititermitinae, and Nasutitermitinae (Mimeutermes); left mandible with apical
tooth (LAt) shorter or longer than fused first plus second marginal tooth (LMt1+2), third (LMt3)
sometimes greatly reduced and represented as a rounded edge (Amitermitinae: Amitermes,
Drepanotermes); right mandible with two marginal teeth (RMt), second marginal (RMt2) reduced
or absent (Apicotermitinae: Jugositermes; Cubitermitinae: Euchilotermes; Termitinae:
Tuberculitermes and Spinitermes). Legs: tarsi tetramerous; tibial spur formula 3:2:2 or 2:2:2,
mesotibia sometimes with additional spines. Cerci dimerous; styli present or absent. Forewing
scale short, not overlapping hindwing scale; wing membranes weakly or not reticulated; venation
as in Rhinotermitidae.
Soldier. Caste absent (Protohamitermitinae, most Apicotermitinae, Amitermitinae: Invasitermes)
or present; head shape variable; monomorphic, dimorphic, or trimorphic; fontanelle present or
rarely absent (e.g., several Apicotermitinae; Amitermitinae: Dentispicotermes, Globitermes;
Bordereau et al.15; Palma-Onetto16; E. Cancello, pers. obs.); mandibles variable, short and stout,
long and slender, or completely reduced as stubs (Nasutitermitinae), with or without teeth; biting
or snapping type (symmetrical or asymmetrical). Antennae with 11-20 antennomeres. Pronotum
saddle-shaped, narrower than head (or subequal to larger in Apicotermitinae: Machadotermes,
Cubitermitinae: Thoracotermes), anterior margin bilobed.
Worker (true worker caste). Crop musculature greatly reduced; gizzard either regressed (without
columnar region, in Apicotermitinae, Cubitermitinae and Engelitermitinae), or complete; gizzard
without sclerotized tooth on columns I and II (unique to Termitidae); stomodeal valve with thin
striations; midgut without caecum; four Malpighian tubules (two in Apicotermitinae:
Labidotermes); no limiting groove between P1 and P2; enteric valve armature within lumen of
valve or everted into P3.

        Remarks: Termitid termites can be assigned to each of the 18 subfamilies using a
combination of criteria from their imago, soldier and worker (see identification key in
Supplementary Note 3). Imago-worker mandibles are most suggestive of their diet, of grinding
type in wood-feeders and pounding type in soil-feeders17. However, they can also be of great
taxonomic value, as extensively put forward by Ahmad18.

        Assignment to a subfamily can be ascertained from the general conformation of workers'
digestive tube (see synthesis presented in Figure 3 in the main text; and Supplementary Figs. 1-
18). One important criterion is the occurrence of a mixed segment (the extension of the mesenteron
over P1), generally present, but absent in Engelitermitinae, Foraminitermitinae,
Forficulitermitinae, Macrotermitinae, Sphaerotermitinae; or either present or absent depending on
the species in Apicotermitinae and Nasutitermitinae. The junction between the mesenteron and P1
occurs ventrally and is elbowed (forming an acute angle) in both Microcerotermitinae and
Neocapritermitinae (Supplementary Figs. 10d,f and 13c), while it is arched (or forming a right or
obtuse angle) in other subfamilies. The shape of P1, although not synapomorphic for any
subfamily, provides additional guidance. In Crepititermitinae, Cylindrotermitinae,

                                   SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION

Engelitermitinae, Forficulitermitinae, Macrotermitinae, Nasutitermitinae, Protohamitermitinae
and Sphaerotermitinae, P1 is always tubular, with a caliber not much different from the
mesenteron. In other subfamilies, the caliber varies depending on the species: it can be tubular or
dilated, reaching a volume equal to or greater than that of the paunch.

        One other main criterion is the insertion of P2 into P3 (i.e., the enteric valve position),
which is generally on the left side of the abdomen, but occurs on the right side in Amitermitinae,
Cylindrotermitinae, Promirotermitinae and Protohamitermitinae; or on either side depending on
species in Apicotermitinae and Nasutitermitinae. The enteric valve armature is generally of interest
for species determination, although it may be of value for assigning them into their subfamily as
well. For example, the valve of Foraminitermitinae is unarmed (Supplementary Fig. 7); the one of
Mirocapritermitinae is formed of six folds (Supplementary Fig. 11), while the one of
Neocapritermitinae exhibits only three folds (Supplementary Fig. 13).

        Although arduous to observe, the insertion of the Malpighian tubules is relevant
phylogenetically4. The four tubules are inserted equidistantly around the junction between the
mesenteron and P1 in Macrotermitinae (see Supplementary Fig. 9d,e) and Sphaerotermitinae. In
the Apicotermitinae, the tubules are located at the middle of the mesenteric tissue. In the other
subfamilies, the tubules are united either in two pairs, or all four are (nearly-)united, at the
boundary between the mesenteric tissue and the foregut. Of note, the Malpighian tubules of
termitid termites are generally tangled in a "knot" sensu Sands2 (Supplementary Fig. 17h). Such
knot is absent in Macrotermitinae and Sphaerotermitinae, while a pseudo-Malpighian knot occurs
in Foraminitermitinae (Supplementary Fig. 7d).

        The occurrence of diverticulum or nodules is of peculiar taxonomic value. Both the
Cubitermitinae and Crepititermitinae exhibit a characteristic diverticulum on P3 (Supplementary
Figs. 3-4). The presence of a Malpighian nodule in Cubitermitinae and Termitinae may be
considered as a synapomorphy, a feature which was secondarily lost in a few species of both
subfamilies (e.g., Lophotermes; 19). This nodule has first been referred as such by Noirot &
Kovoor20, as "Malpighian sac" by Bignell et al.21, or Malpighian "diverticulum" by Sands22. These
nodules are distinct from "ampulla(e)", which are dilatation of the proximal part of the Malpighian
tubules. For instance, Tuberculitermes (Termitinae) exhibits four Malpighian tubules: they are
tubular throughout, or can each dilate proximally into an ampulla; the tubules are attached in pairs,
each with a united base, attached on either a true diverticulum (i.e., the nodule) or not22. In
Prohamitermes hosei (Amitermitinae), the tubules are not attached in pairs, and the four tubules
each end into their own globular ampulla (Supplementary Fig. 1f); note, however, that these
ampullae are referred to as "nodules" by Noirot4. On the other hand, Angulitermes
(Promirotermitinae) either shows ampullae or none, and does not exhibit a nodule.
